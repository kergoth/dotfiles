# Franchise Research

Identify which entries deserve play today, the best entry point, and a minimal
set preserving worthwhile content without unnecessary repetition. Ask which
franchise only if missing.

## Coverage

Account for known mainline games, sequels, prequels, and notable spin-offs sharing
core gameplay. Consider remakes, remasters, rereleases, compilations, and relevant
alternative implementations. Enumerate entries before selecting recommendations:
the platform mode's two-source threshold does not determine coverage here.

Set explicit boundaries for unusually large franchises. Group minor editions and
unrelated spin-offs with reasons; list known material exclusions and unresolved
gaps. Do not claim exhaustive discovery of every regional SKU or fan project.

For each entry, assess design, controls, pacing, friction, whether it stands on
its own, suitability as an entry point, and redundancy. Assign Essential,
Worthwhile, Optional / For Fans, Skip, or Avoid with reasons. Distinguish lack of
evidence from a negative assessment.

Compare versions and implementations for shortlisted entries; avoid duplicating
full version reports for every minor release. Identify practical setup and access,
including delisting, bundles split into separate installs, required original
data, and edition-specific content.

## Replacement and content coverage

For material comparisons, distinguish:

- Fully superseded under stated criteria: worthwhile mechanics/content and the
  relevant experience are effectively covered by the replacement.
- Mechanical improvements with unique earlier content: explain improvements
  without treating a subjective preference as objective supersession.
- Not superseded: earlier content or experience remains independently valuable.

Verify whether sequels or compilations actually include earlier content. Report
partial overlap and unique material, including aesthetic or mechanical changes
that make a remake an alternative rather than a replacement. Include a concise
content matrix when overlap changes what the user should play.

## Output

Lead with whether the franchise suits the requested experience and who it fits.
Then provide ranked recommendations:

| Rank | Entry | Category | Why play and caveats | Best release/setup | Community evidence | Overlap/replacement | Sources |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Account for remaining scoped entries in a compact coverage table; a skipped game
still belongs in the coverage record. Include the content matrix when needed.

Provide the best single entry point and minimal play set, stating what unique
content or experience the smaller set omits. Distinguish recommendation ranking
from suggested play order when story continuity or mechanics make order matter.

Finish with important version/access notes, citations, research date and scope,
conflicting evidence, and material coverage gaps. Verify that every scoped entry
is accounted for and that minimal-set claims match actual content inclusion.

Use this schema for scoped entries outside the ranked recommendations:

| Entry or grouped variants | Disposition | Reason, unique content, and gaps | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|

For a content matrix, use releases as rows and the decision-relevant campaigns,
levels, modes, or other content groups as columns. Distinguish full, partial,
absent, and unknown coverage.
