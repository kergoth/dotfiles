# Source Selection and Port Discovery

Source guidance reviewed: 2026-10-27. Names and links are starting points,
not a permanent whitelist or a claim that every source has been verified for
the current task. Recheck accessibility, relevance, and evidence quality at use.

## Match sources to claims

| Purpose | Useful starting points | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoyment today | Platform-specific Reddit communities, r/patientgamers, relevant genre forums, substantive GameFAQs discussions | Isolated reviews are anecdotes; specialist enthusiasm need not generalize |
| Broader community patterns | Transparent polls, retrospective aggregates, Backloggd | Inspect participation, sample bias, and edition aggregation; upvotes are not consensus percentages |
| Candidate discovery | Relevant Nintendo Life, Time Extension, IGN, GamesRadar, Retro Dodo, or Giant Bomb coverage | Evaluate the actual article; historical lists and broad ratings do not establish current playability |
| Version differences | Hardcore Gaming 101, Digital Foundry/DF Retro, Battle of the Ports, PCGamingWiki, specialist communities | Confirm exact versions and distinguish measured defects from preferences |
| Release identity and content | Official manuals, release documentation, MobyGames, relevant The Cutting Room Floor pages | Unused/cut assets are not necessarily playable or worthwhile content |
| Compatibility and access | Emulator/interpreter documentation, compatibility trackers, upstream releases/issues, publisher notices, exact store pages | Verify game, host, build, region, date, and required data; framework support does not prove game support |

Treat broad r/gaming discussions, Metacritic aggregates, and generic listicles as
supplementary rather than default authorities. Prefer relevant detailed evidence
over publication reputation. Backloggd may aggregate editions into a parent game;
use edition-specific evidence where available. Keep ratings separate from counts.

## Discover alternative implementations

For runtime discovery, combine the originating platform and target host with
terms such as emulator, compatibility layer, fork, release, renderer, and the
device or GPU name. Follow new-project leads to upstream release notes. Match
the build used in a performance report to the project's release timeline,
especially across core/renderer changes that may invalidate an older bottleneck
explanation. Use this alongside game-specific port discovery below.

- [Quiver Launcher](https://quiverlauncher.com/): community catalog for ports,
  recompilations, and recreations. Follow entries to upstream releases; listing
  and installability alone do not establish completeness or recommendation quality.
- Video Game Esoterica on YouTube: discover recent projects and inspect demonstrated
  gameplay. Verify the channel and exact video during research; follow project
  links upstream. Separate titles/descriptions, announcements, short demonstrations,
  and reports of extended play. Do not infer full-game reliability from a clip.
- [GameTechWiki's notable ports](https://emulation.gametechwiki.com/index.php/List_of_notable_ports)
  and relevant emulation wiki pages: discovery leads and technical context. Verify
  material claims against project documentation and independent play evidence.
- [Harbour Masters](https://github.com/HarbourMasters): established developer example
  deserving serious consideration. Use documented track record as supporting
  evidence, then check each project, release, host, and separately maintained fork.
- Upstream project pages and relevant preservation/port communities: look for
  lesser-known implementations, competing projects with similar names, and recent
  releases missing from general rankings.

Search the game title and regional aliases with terms such as source port,
decompilation, decomp, recompilation, recomp, reimplementation, remake, remaster,
translation, and patch as relevant. Follow credible leads rather than treating
every keyword as a mandatory query. Check for competing forks and similarly named
projects before assigning evidence or recommendations.

An announcement repeated by a video, news article, and catalog is one underlying
claim. Independent firsthand testing adds evidence; repeated coverage does not.
Use older detailed comparisons for stable differences and fresh checks for
releases, compatibility, availability, maintenance concerns, and service status.
